ONDC for Restaurants: How to Join, Real Costs and Results

ONDC for restaurants in India: how to join through a seller app, what it really costs per order (with a ₹ example), and what results to expect in 2026.

POSINEXT Editorial Team24 November 2026 · 12 min read

ONDC, the Open Network for Digital Commerce, is the government-backed network that lets a restaurant sell through many ordering apps at once without signing a separate deal with each one. For a restaurant paying high aggregator commissions, the pitch is attractive: lower fees, your own prices and discounts, and more say over delivery and customer data. The reality in late 2026 is more mixed. ONDC can add cheaper orders, but demand on the network has moved up and down with subsidies, and the savings per order are smaller than the headline numbers once delivery is counted.

This guide explains how ONDC works for restaurants, how to join step by step, what it really costs (with a worked ₹ example), and what results are realistic. It is the ONDC chapter of our guide to restaurant marketing channels in India.

What ONDC is, in plain terms

ONDC is an initiative of the Department for Promotion of Industry and Internal Trade (DPIIT), Government of India, and is run as a not-for-profit company under Section 8 of the Companies Act, 2013. It is not an app you download. It is a set of shared rules and connections, similar in spirit to UPI, that lets different apps talk to each other. A Ministry of Commerce and Industry overview published on PIB in January 2025 lists Food & Beverage as one of the network's domains, with categories such as fast food, desserts, beverages and North Indian.

There are three kinds of players a restaurant needs to understand:

  • Buyer apps are where customers browse and order. Over the years these have included Paytm, Magicpin and Ola (named by Inc42 in 2025). PhonePe's Pincode exited ONDC, according to Business Standard in March 2025.
  • Seller apps (seller network participants) are where your restaurant lists its menu, prices and timings and receives orders. Most restaurants join ONDC through one of these rather than directly.
  • Logistics providers deliver the order if you do not use your own riders.

The key difference from Swiggy or Zomato is that your one listing on a seller app can be visible on every participating buyer app. The National Restaurant Association of India (NRAI) has promoted this as giving control back to restaurants: in an Economic Times interview reprinted on the ONDC blog, NRAI trustee Anurag Katriar said access to data, discounting and choice of delivery fleet would be the restaurant's own decision on ONDC.

Where ONDC food delivery stands in 2026

Before you plan around ONDC, look at the track record honestly.

  • The subsidy boom and slowdown. Business Standard reported in March 2025 (citing The Economic Times) that ONDC cut its monthly incentive cap from ₹3 crore in July 2024 to about ₹30 lakh, and that retail orders on the network, which include food delivery, fell from a peak of 6.5 million in October 2024 to 4.6 million in February 2025. Inc42 reported around the same time that food delivery volumes had been flat since the 2024 peak and that buyer apps had cut food discounts.
  • ONDC's own claims. ONDC's food and beverages page currently says it has 100K+ active restaurants and that commissions are reduced "from ~25–35% to ~8–11%". It also claims a 31-minute average delivery time and 95–98% fill rates. These are the network's own marketing figures, not independent audits.
  • New entrants are using the network. Outlook Business reported in August 2026 (citing Moneycontrol) that Flipkart planned to start food delivery in Bengaluru on ONDC with a commission of about 10%, against roughly 16–30% on existing platforms. TechCrunch reported on 19 October 2026 that the service was still in employee testing, drawing on restaurants listed through ONDC plus restaurants Flipkart signed directly, and that Flipkart had told restaurants it expected a public launch by end-October and five or six cities by the end of 2026. Check whether it is live in your city before counting on it.
  • Zero-commission rivals outside ONDC. Rapido's Ownly, which Inc42 reported in August 2025 had joined the ONDC push on a zero-commission model, moved inside the main Rapido app in July 2026 with close to 20,000 restaurants and no commission, according to Business Today. Treat Ownly as a separate channel with its own sign-up.

The takeaway: ONDC is a real, low-cost channel, but order volume depends heavily on which buyer apps are active and promoting food in your city at the time. Plan for it as an extra channel, not a replacement for aggregators.

How to join ONDC as a restaurant: step by step

ONDC describes four routes for sellers, according to the onboarding checklist published by Nagaland's Ease of Doing Business portal: join through a seller app that aggregates sellers; become a network participant yourself with your own technology; become a participant and use a technology service provider; or fill in ONDC's interest form. Becoming a participant yourself involves ONDC portal registration with e-KYC, certified staff, API integration and staging, pre-production and production testing, as the integration guide hosted by Goa's Investment Promotion Board (Goa IPB) sets out. That is built for software companies and large chains. For a single restaurant or a small group, the practical route is a seller app.

  1. Get your documents ready. Keep your FSSAI licence or registration, GST registration (if registered), PAN, bank account details for settlements, outlet address and opening hours, and a clean menu with prices, photos, veg/non-veg marking and packaging charges. Seller apps set their own document lists, so ask yours for the exact requirements.
  2. Shortlist two or three seller apps. ONDC's site links to its live registry of network participants. Compare them on the questions in the checklist below, especially fees, settlement time and whether they connect to your POS.
  3. Decide who delivers. Choose between your own riders (cheaper on short distances, but you manage staff) and the logistics partners the seller app offers. Get the per-order delivery price for your typical distance in writing.
  4. Set your ONDC prices and discounts. You control these on ONDC. Many restaurants keep menu prices the same as dine-in and use small, capped offers rather than deep discounts.
  5. Go live and test. Place two or three test orders from different buyer apps. Check that the order reaches your kitchen, the KOT prints or shows on the kitchen display, the rider is assigned, and the payment reaches your bank on time.
  6. Track results for 30 days using the weekly numbers in the last section, then decide whether to push harder or keep ONDC as a small extra channel.

Questions to ask a seller app before you sign

  • What is your fee per order, and is it a percentage, a flat fee or a subscription? Is GST charged on top?
  • What fee do the buyer apps charge, and is it deducted from my payout?
  • Will you pass on the ONDC network fee to me, and how will it appear on my statement?
  • What does delivery cost for 2 km, 4 km and 6 km, and who pays if the rider is late or the order is cancelled?
  • How many days until settlement reaches my bank, and do you provide an order-wise statement?
  • Which buyer apps actually show my restaurant today, in my city?
  • Do you integrate with my POS, or will my staff re-enter orders by hand?
  • Can I leave at any time, and is there a minimum period or setup fee?

What ONDC costs a restaurant

On Swiggy or Zomato, most of your cost is a single commission that usually bundles delivery. On ONDC the cost is split across several parties, and the exact split depends on your seller app, the buyer app and the delivery choice.

Two lessons from this example. First, when the restaurant pays for delivery, the ONDC saving shrinks to about ₹11 per order, and a ₹20 discount you fund to attract ONDC customers would wipe it out. Second, the saving becomes large only when delivery is cheap (short distances, your own riders) or the customer pays for it. That is why your delivery setup matters more than the commission rate when you evaluate ONDC.

Getting orders once you are live

Listing alone rarely brings volume. Restaurants that see results usually treat ONDC as a channel they actively promote:

  • Price sensibly. Keep ONDC prices close to your dine-in menu. If aggregator prices are marked up to cover commission, ONDC can show a lower price to the same customer.
  • Get the menu right. Clear names, photos of your bestsellers, correct veg/non-veg tags and short descriptions matter on every buyer app.
  • Use small, capped offers. Inc42 reported in 2025 that ONDC food delivery began with prices "almost 50% cheaper" than Zomato and Swiggy because of subsidies, and that volumes stalled when those subsidies shrank. Do not build your plan on deep discounts that you cannot sustain.
  • Keep ratings high. Late or wrong orders hurt you on every buyer app. Watch prep times at peak hours.
  • Do not neglect your own channels. For regulars, your own online ordering website is usually cheaper still, because there is no buyer app or seller app fee. See our comparison of direct ordering vs aggregators.

Running ONDC orders without chaos in the kitchen

Every extra channel adds a tablet, a login and a payout statement. Before adding ONDC, make sure orders from all channels land in one place, print the right KOT, and deduct stock from the same inventory. Ask your seller app whether it can push orders to your POS. A POS like POSINEXT already brings Swiggy and Zomato orders and your own website orders into one billing screen with GST invoices and KOTs, which keeps the counter calm when you add more channels.

Reconcile every week. Match each ONDC order to its settlement, and check that fees match what the seller app promised. Our guide to aggregator payout reconciliation walks through the method, and it works the same way for ONDC statements.

What results to expect, and how to judge them

Be realistic. Public sources do not give reliable restaurant-level order numbers for ONDC, and network volumes have swung with incentives. A sensible expectation is that ONDC becomes a small extra source of delivery orders, with better margin per order than aggregators if your delivery cost is under control. Results will vary widely by city, cuisine and which buyer apps are active.

Track these numbers every week for the first 30 days:

  • ONDC orders per week, and the share coming from each buyer app.
  • Average order value compared with Swiggy and Zomato.
  • Total cost per order (all fees, delivery, discounts and GST on fees) as a percentage of food value.
  • Late, cancelled or complained-about orders.
  • Repeat customers, if your seller app shares that data.

If cost per order is clearly lower than on aggregators and orders are growing, promote it. If orders stay near zero after a month of decent prices and a clean menu, keep the listing live at low effort and put your energy into channels that are working.

Is ONDC right for your restaurant?

ONDC tends to suit restaurants with a strong local following, delivery-friendly food, their own riders or cheap delivery nearby, and the discipline to track numbers by channel. It is a weaker fit for restaurants that depend on aggregator discovery, have long delivery distances, or cannot spare staff to manage another channel. Frequently asked questions

Is there a fee to join ONDC as a restaurant?

ONDC does not sell listings to restaurants directly. You join through a seller app, which sets its own fees (percentage, flat or subscription), so ask for the full fee list, including buyer app fees and any network fee pass-through, before you sign.

How much commission does ONDC charge restaurants?

There is no single rate. ONDC's own food page claims commissions of about 8–11%, and Inc42 reported buyer apps like Paytm, Ola and Magicpin taking about 5%. Add your seller app fee, delivery and GST on fees to get your real cost per order.

Which apps show my restaurant if I join ONDC?

Participating buyer apps, which change over time and vary by city. ONDC publishes a live registry of network participants on ondc.org; ask your seller app which buyer apps are actually showing your outlet today.

Do I need my own delivery riders for ONDC?

No. Most seller apps offer logistics partners. Your own riders can be cheaper on short distances, and since delivery is often the largest cost on an ONDC order, compare both before you decide.

Should I leave Swiggy and Zomato if I join ONDC?

Usually not. ONDC order volumes have depended on subsidies and on which buyer apps are active in your city. Treat it as an extra lower-cost channel and judge it on 30 days of real numbers.

Is Rapido's Ownly the same as ONDC?

No. Ownly is Rapido's own food delivery service inside the Rapido app, with its own restaurant sign-up and, per Business Today, zero commission. Inc42 reported in 2025 that Rapido joined the ONDC push, but you should treat Ownly as a separate channel.
