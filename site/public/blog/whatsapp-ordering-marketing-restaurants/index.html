WhatsApp Ordering and Marketing for Restaurants in India

WhatsApp ordering and marketing for restaurants in India: opt-in rules, 2026 message costs, a 7-step ordering setup, reorder nudges and a ₹ cost example.

POSINEXT Editorial Team23 November 2026 · 12 min read

Your regulars already have WhatsApp open all day. Many of them already message you to ask "are you open?", "is the biryani available?" or "can you send the menu?". The question is not whether to use WhatsApp, but how to turn those chats into orders and repeat visits without spamming people, breaking WhatsApp's rules or drowning your staff in messages during the dinner rush.

This guide covers both halves: ordering (taking orders through WhatsApp without mistakes) and marketing (building an opted-in list, sending broadcasts and getting reorders). It includes what WhatsApp actually charges in India today, a sample opt-in line, a cost-per-order example in rupees and a 30-day plan. It is the WhatsApp chapter of our guide to restaurant marketing channels in India.

Why WhatsApp works for restaurants in India

India is WhatsApp's biggest market. TechCrunch, writing in December 2025, described it as used by more than 500 million people in India, and cited Sensor Tower data that 94% of WhatsApp's monthly users in India opened the app daily in November 2025, against 59% in the US. The same report said that 67% of WhatsApp Business users in India opened that app daily, and that WhatsApp Business monthly users in India were up more than 130% compared with 2021.

For a restaurant, that means two things. Customers will see your message, and they are comfortable ordering or asking questions in chat. The flip side is that people are quick to mute, block or report businesses that message too often, and WhatsApp uses that feedback to limit what a business can send. Getting results on WhatsApp is about relevance and permission, not volume.

There are two ways to run WhatsApp for your restaurant. Most single outlets should start with the free-to-download Business app and move to the Platform only when volume or automation demands it.

It also lists paid options: Meta Verified and ads that click to WhatsApp. Some features, such as Business Broadcasts with scheduling and buttons, are marked as available to eligible users in select countries, so check what your app shows.

What WhatsApp messaging costs in India (late 2026)

On the Business app, ordinary chats carry no per-message charge. On the Business Platform, Meta's pricing page (updated 30 September 2026) says that since 1 July 2025 Meta charges per delivered message, based on the message category and the customer's country code. The categories that matter to a restaurant are:

  • Marketing: offers, new dishes, festival menus, "we miss you" reorder nudges.
  • Utility: order confirmed, out for delivery, booking reminder.
  • Authentication: one-time passwords, for example to verify a phone number.
  • Service: your free-form replies after a customer messages you.

A customer service window of 24 hours opens every time a customer messages you, and resets with each new message. Inside it you can reply freely; outside it you can only start a conversation with an approved template. Meta's page says service messages were charged again from 1 October 2026, with 1,000 free service messages per business phone number per month, and that utility messages inside an open window are charged again from the same date. Messages in a "free entry point" window, opened when someone messages you from a click-to-WhatsApp ad, are not charged for marketing, utility, authentication or service messages.

Meta publishes the actual rates in its rate cards. As reported by the Free Press Journal on 30 September 2026, citing providers that track Meta's rate card, India's rates from 1 October 2026 were:

CategoryReported rate per delivered message (India, before GST)

Utility₹0.115

IBTimes India reported the same ₹0.115 figure for utility, authentication and service messages. Providers add 18% GST and usually their own platform fee. Meta says it changes rates only at the start of a quarter, and India's marketing rate already went up on 1 January 2026, so check the current rate card or your provider before budgeting.

Set up WhatsApp ordering in seven steps

The biggest problem with WhatsApp ordering is not getting orders; it is getting them right. Free-text orders ("2 paneer tikka, one less spicy, and that thing from last time") lead to wrong items, missed notes and arguments about price. Structure everything you can.

  1. Use a separate business number. Keep it off the owner's personal phone so that staff can take over and the number survives staff changes.
  2. Complete the business profile. Add your address, hours, delivery area, website and a short description such as "Order before 9:30 pm for delivery within 4 km".
  3. Build the catalog by menu section. Add bestsellers first with a photo, price and portion size. Group items into collections (Starters, Mains, Combos). Customers can add items to a cart and send it as one message, which is far clearer than free text.
  4. Write a greeting and an away message. The greeting should give the three things people need: the menu link, timings and how to order. The away message should say when you open and link to your ordering website so the order is not lost.
  5. Save quick replies. Create shortcuts for the menu, delivery charges, payment options, order confirmation and "out for delivery". Consistent wording prevents mistakes.
  6. Use labels as an order board. For example: New order, Confirmed, Paid, Out for delivery, Completed. At the end of a shift, nothing should sit under "New order".
  7. Confirm every order in one message. Repeat the items, notes, total, payment method, address and expected time, and ask the customer to reply "OK". Then enter it in your POS so a KOT reaches the kitchen and the sale lands in your reports.

Step seven is where WhatsApp ordering usually breaks at scale: someone has to re-type every chat order into the billing system. Once you get more than a handful of chat orders a day, send customers a link to an ordering website instead. With POSINEXT's online ordering website, the menu and prices come from the POS and each order lands in the POS with its KOT, so WhatsApp becomes the place where you share the link and answer questions, not where you key in orders.

Build an opted-in list the right way

WhatsApp's Business Messaging Policy says you may only contact people if they gave you their number and you received opt-in permission confirming they want messages from you. Meta's opt-in guide adds that the opt-in must clearly say the person is agreeing to receive messages from your business and must clearly name the business. You choose how to collect it, on a website, by SMS, in person or on paper, as long as it complies with applicable law. You must also honour opt-out requests made on or off WhatsApp.

India's data protection law points the same way. The Digital Personal Data Protection Rules, 2025 were notified on 14 November 2025, and the PIB explainer says every data fiduciary must issue a consent notice that is clear and easy to understand, and that people may withdraw consent at any time. India Briefing's summary notes that core obligations such as consent notices apply after an 18-month phase-in, but it is simplest to collect consent properly from day one. This is general information, not legal advice; read the official text on the MeitY and PIB websites.

Where to collect opt-ins:

  • A table tent or counter standee with a QR code that opens a chat with a pre-filled message such as "Yes, send me offers".
  • A line on the printed bill and on delivery bag stickers.
  • A checkbox (unticked by default) on your ordering website.
  • Staff asking at payment: "Can we WhatsApp you our weekly specials? You can stop any time."

Sample opt-in wording:

Get weekly specials and festival menus from [Your Restaurant] on WhatsApp. About 2 messages a month. Reply STOP any time to opt out.

Meta's guide recommends separate opt-ins for different kinds of messages, such as order updates versus offers, because people who get only what they asked for block businesses less. Record when and how each person opted in, and remove anyone who says stop.

What to send, and how often

A good rule is that every message should be something the customer would be happy to have received. For most restaurants that means:

  • Order updates (utility): confirmed, being prepared, out for delivery. Short and expected.
  • A weekly or fortnightly special (marketing): one dish, one photo, a price and an order link.
  • Festival and event menus: sent early enough for pre-orders.
  • Reorder nudges: to customers who have not ordered in, say, 30 days, with their usual item if you know it.
  • Booking reminders: for table reservations and party orders.

Respect the limits. Meta's developer documentation says WhatsApp may cap how many marketing template messages a person receives from all businesses in a period, based on how often that person reads marketing messages; blocked messages fail with an error, and Meta advises waiting at least 24 hours before resending. TechCrunch has also reported WhatsApp testing ways for people to stop specific categories such as offers, and testing paid, customisable broadcasts on the Business app. In practice, one or two marketing messages a month to an engaged list will beat daily blasts to everyone.

Avoid content the policy prohibits. Alcohol is a regulated category: it is not allowed on the Business app at all, and on the Platform only in permitted countries, with licences and age gating. If you run a bar, promote food and events, and check the current policy before mentioning drinks.

Now change one assumption: if only 1% of messages turn into orders, you get 12 orders and the cost per order rises to about ₹269, which is worse than many aggregator orders. The lesson is that list quality decides everything. A smaller list of real regulars who asked to hear from you beats a big list of numbers collected without consent.

If you run the Business app instead of the Platform, the messaging cost is close to zero but the cost moves to staff time, so count the minutes spent per chat order too.

Common mistakes

  • Adding everyone from the billing system to a broadcast. A phone number on a bill is not an opt-in.
  • Sending the same discount every week. Customers learn to wait for it. Rotate dishes and give reasons to order, not just discounts.
  • Taking orders on the owner's phone. Orders get missed when the owner is busy or away.
  • Not entering chat orders in the POS. The kitchen misses tickets, and your sales and stock reports go wrong.
  • Relying on WhatsApp Web all day. TechCrunch reported in December 2025 that government directions issued on 28 November 2025 require messaging apps to stay tied to the SIM and to log out web and desktop sessions every six hours. Check how this applies when you plan who handles chats on which device.

Your 30-day WhatsApp plan

  1. Week 1: set up the business number, profile, catalog of top 20 items, greeting, away message and quick replies.
  2. Week 2: print opt-in QR codes for tables, counter and delivery bags; add the opt-in line to bills; brief staff on the one-sentence ask.
  3. Week 3: send your first special to opted-in customers, with an ordering link. Track orders and opt-outs.
  4. Week 4: review the numbers, decide your monthly message rhythm, and decide whether volume now justifies the Business Platform and an ordering website that feeds your POS.

Frequently asked questions

Is WhatsApp Business free for restaurants?

The WhatsApp Business app has no per-message charge for normal chats. The WhatsApp Business Platform (API) charges per delivered message by category, and in India marketing messages were reported at about ₹0.86 each before GST from October 2026, plus your provider's fee.

Can I send offers to every customer whose number is on my bills?

No. WhatsApp's policy allows you to message people only if they gave you their number and opted in to messages from your business. Collect a clear opt-in that names your restaurant, and remove anyone who asks to stop.

How often should a restaurant send WhatsApp marketing messages?

Most restaurants do well with one or two marketing messages a month plus order updates. WhatsApp may limit marketing messages to people who rarely read them, and frequent blasts lead to blocks that can restrict your account.

Should I take orders in WhatsApp chat or send an ordering link?

Chat orders are fine at low volume if you confirm every order and enter it in your POS. As volume grows, send a link to an ordering website so items, prices and notes arrive structured and print as a KOT without re-typing.

Can a bar promote drinks on WhatsApp?

Alcohol is a restricted category in WhatsApp's Business Messaging Policy. It is not allowed on the Business app, and on the Platform only in permitted countries with licences and age gating, so check the current policy and promote food and events instead.
