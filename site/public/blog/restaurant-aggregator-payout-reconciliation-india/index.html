How to Reconcile Swiggy & Zomato Payouts

Match aggregator orders, fees, refunds and bank credits with a practical Swiggy and Zomato settlement reconciliation checklist for Indian restaurants.

By the POSINEXT team · 20 November 2026

Good delivery sales do not automatically mean the right amount reached your bank account. The settlement statement may include restaurant-funded discounts, platform promotions, commissions, service fees, advertising, refunds, taxes on platform fees and other adjustments. If you compare only the final payout with your POS sales, it is difficult to identify which line caused a difference.

This guide gives Indian restaurant owners, QSR operators and cloud kitchens a repeatable process to reconcile Swiggy and Zomato settlements against POS orders, platform statements, invoices and actual bank credits. It is an operational checklist—not a claim that every platform uses the same statement fields or settlement cycle.

What restaurant payout reconciliation actually checks

Reconciliation is the process of matching three records: the orders and sales recorded in your POS, the platform's settlement statement and invoices, and the amount actually credited to your bank. The purpose is to explain every difference, not just to make the totals appear equal.

  • Order completeness: Do completed orders in the POS appear in the platform report, and vice versa?
  • Order status: Are cancelled, refunded, rejected or disputed orders classified consistently?
  • Discount ownership: Which promotions were funded by your restaurant and which were funded by the platform?
  • Fees: Are commission, advertising, service, payment or other charges consistent with the agreement and the invoice?
  • Settlement: Does the net amount in the statement match the bank credit, including split or delayed transfers?

1. Download the records for the same settlement period

Before doing any calculation, collect the records that cover the same dates and outlet. Save the original files so you can raise a query later without relying on screenshots or memory.

  • POS channel sales report for Swiggy and Zomato, including order IDs and order status
  • Platform order-level report for the settlement period
  • Settlement or payout statement, including adjustments
  • Fee, advertising or other service invoices and supporting statements
  • Bank statement showing the payout credit (and any separate credit or debit)
  • Copies of the current commercial agreement and any approved promotion or campaign terms

Use the platform's settlement or payout identifier to connect a statement to its bank transfer. Do not assume that the order date, settlement date and bank-credit date are the same.

2. Match orders by ID—not just by daily total

Daily totals can hide missing or duplicated entries. Match each order using its platform order ID wherever possible. Use a spreadsheet and keep the platform ID as a text value so long IDs are not rounded or reformatted.

Field to compareWhat to check
Platform order IDUnique order appears once; duplicate or missing rows are flagged
Order date and timeUse the same time zone and the platform's reporting convention
POS channelCorrect outlet and aggregator channel
Order statusDelivered, cancelled, refunded or adjusted status agrees across records
Item totalMenu item value matches after documented changes
DiscountsSeparate restaurant-funded and platform-funded promotions when the report identifies them
Packaging and other chargesCompare the recorded amounts and their treatment in each report
Refunds or adjustmentsRecord reason, order ID, amount and the period in which the adjustment appears

Do not force a match where a platform uses a different reporting date or a later adjustment. Mark it as an exception and follow the transaction through the next statement.

3. Reconcile every deduction against an agreement or invoice

Do not assume a single commission percentage applies to all orders. The fee basis, rate, promotions, service charges and other commercial terms can differ by outlet, program and contract. Check each deduction against the applicable agreement and invoice.

  • Commission or service fee: Verify the feeable amount, applicable rate and order exclusions against your agreement.
  • GST on platform services: Match the tax shown on the platform's fee invoice. Do not treat the GST on a platform service invoice as the GST on the restaurant's food order.
  • Restaurant-funded discount: Check that the amount matches the promotion you approved and the platform's statement.
  • Platform-funded discount: Verify whether and how the platform credits its share; do not automatically treat the entire discount as a restaurant cost.
  • Advertising or sponsored listing: Match the campaign, dates, budget, approval and charges. Keep proof of the approval or cancellation.
  • Refund, cancellation or dispute: Check the order, reason, status and supporting evidence; avoid assuming a refund is valid or invalid without records.
  • Other adjustments: Ask for a supporting transaction or invoice reference for charges you cannot identify.

4. Understand the GST distinction for aggregator food orders

In India, GST on specified restaurant services supplied through electronic commerce operators is handled under section 9(5) of the CGST Act, with the platform responsible for paying that GST. CBIC's Circular No. 167/23/2021-GST dated 17 December 2021 explains compliance and reporting modalities for restaurant services supplied through e-commerce operators.

This is separate from the GST invoiced on the platform's own services to the restaurant, such as fees or other charges. Keep the platform's fee invoice and your restaurant-sales records distinct, and ask your accountant how the platform order data should be reported in your returns. The applicable treatment and reporting should be checked for your business and current rules.

Read Circular 167/23/2021-GST (official PDF).

5. Calculate the expected settlement—with an illustrative example

The statement's structure and deduction order may vary by platform and contract. The following is a simplified illustration, not a platform's official formula, fee schedule or forecast.

Illustrative lineAmount
Completed order value included in statement₹20,000.00
Restaurant-funded discounts−₹1,200.00
Refunds / adjustments in this settlement−₹600.00
Net sales after these deductions₹18,200.00
Platform-funded promotional credit (illustrative)+₹500.00
Platform service fee (illustrative 10% of ₹18,200)−₹1,820.00
GST on that fee (illustrative 18%)−₹327.60
Advertising charge supported by a statement−₹300.00
Other documented adjustment−₹150.00
Illustrative expected payout₹16,102.40

In this illustration, the arithmetic is ₹18,200 + ₹500 − ₹1,820 − ₹327.60 − ₹300 − ₹150 = ₹16,102.40. The assumed fee rate and promotional credit are only examples. Use your actual contract, statement and invoices. If a deduction is not supported, leave it in the exception list rather than inventing a balancing entry.

6. Match the statement to the bank

Once the statement's net payout is calculated, match it to the bank credit using the transfer reference, date and amount. If one statement is paid in multiple transfers, or several statements are combined into one credit, create a mapping table to link the components.

  • Statement net payout
  • Bank credit amount
  • Bank value date and transfer reference
  • Difference, if any
  • Reason and follow-up status

Keep timing differences separate from unexplained differences. A transfer that arrives in the next bank day is not necessarily a loss; it still needs to be tracked until it clears.

7. Create an exceptions register and raise queries with evidence

Every mismatch should have an owner, amount, order or statement reference, evidence and a next action. Avoid sending a vague request such as “payout is low.” Submit a specific list of disputed rows.

ExceptionEvidence to attachAction
Missing orderPOS order ID, platform order report, date and valueAsk the platform to trace the order and settlement
Wrong cancellation / refundOrder status, kitchen/POS record and refund detailsRequest the status history and reason
Unrecognised campaign deductionCampaign name, approval record, invoice and statementAsk for the activation record and charge basis
Fee rate or base mismatchAgreement, order breakdown, invoice and calculationAsk for a fee calculation by order or settlement
Bank-credit mismatchPayout reference, statement and bank statementRequest transfer trace or explanation of split settlement

Keep the ticket or email number and follow the query until a credit, reversal, explanation or closure is recorded. Do not delete the original discrepancy from your worksheet when the platform responds; record the resolution.

8. Use a weekly reconciliation routine

  1. Download reports and invoices for each outlet and platform.
  2. Match order IDs and statuses against POS channel sales.
  3. Verify discounts, refunds, fees, taxes on fees and campaign deductions.
  4. Calculate the expected net payout from the statement.
  5. Match expected payouts to bank credits.
  6. Open a query for each unexplained difference and attach evidence.
  7. Review unresolved exceptions and confirmed adjustments with the owner or accountant.

Weekly reconciliation is easier to manage than waiting until month-end, when order details, approvals and staff recollections may be harder to retrieve. If you run multiple outlets, reconcile each outlet and platform separately before consolidating.

What a restaurant POS should make easier

Your POS should clearly separate online order sales by channel, preserve order identifiers where available, show cancelled or adjusted orders, and export date-filtered reports. Those records are the starting point for settlement reconciliation; the platform's statement and bank records remain necessary to verify payout deductions and credits.

Frequently asked questions

How often should a restaurant reconcile Swiggy and Zomato payouts?

A weekly routine is a practical starting point for most single outlets: match order IDs and statuses, deductions, statement totals and bank credits, then track unresolved differences. Higher-volume outlets may reconcile more frequently.

What records do I need to reconcile an aggregator payout?

Keep POS channel sales and order IDs, platform order-level reports, settlement statements, fee and advertising invoices, approved campaign records, bank credits and the current commercial agreement.

Why is my aggregator payout lower than POS sales?

The settlement may reflect restaurant-funded promotions, commissions or other service fees, GST on platform fees, refunds, cancellation adjustments, advertising charges or timing differences. Match each line to the agreement, statement and invoice instead of assuming a single reason.

Does the restaurant pay GST again on Swiggy or Zomato restaurant orders?

Specified restaurant services supplied through e-commerce operators are covered by section 9(5), under which the operator pays the GST on the restaurant service. The restaurant's platform-service invoices are separate. Reporting and compliance should be confirmed with your accountant.

How do I dispute an unexplained settlement deduction?

Prepare the order or payout reference, amount, date, POS and platform records, invoice or campaign approval evidence, and a specific question. Submit it through the platform's partner support channel and track the case until it is resolved.
