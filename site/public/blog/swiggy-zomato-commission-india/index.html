Swiggy and Zomato Commission: Real Cost and Margin per Order

Swiggy and Zomato commission explained for Indian restaurants: every fee, GST and TDS line, a ₹420 worked example and a formula to price your delivery menu.

By the POSINEXT team · 21 November 2026

Ask ten restaurant owners what Swiggy or Zomato charges them and you will hear ten different numbers. That is not because anyone is wrong. The commission (now usually called a service fee) is negotiated per outlet, it is charged on a base defined in your contract, and several smaller fees and taxes sit on top of it. The number that matters is not the headline percentage. It is how much of a ₹420 order actually reaches your bank, and how much is left after food and packaging.

This guide shows what the deductions are, what has been reported publicly about rates and new fees, and how to calculate your real cost and margin per order with a worked example. It is the delivery chapter of our guide to restaurant marketing channels in India.

What Swiggy and Zomato actually deduct

Your partner agreement and the weekly payout statement are the only reliable source for your own rates. Every deduction you see will fall into one of these buckets:

DeductionWhat it isWhat to check in your contract
Commission / service feeA percentage of the order value, charged for listing, ordering and (usually) deliveryThe rate, and the base: item total only, or gross value including packaging and taxes
Payment or collection feeA percentage charged for collecting the customer's paymentRate and whether it applies to every order
Distance or long-distance feeA per-order charge for deliveries beyond a set radiusDistance bands, amounts, minimum order value

Restaurant-funded discountsYour share of offers shown to customersWhich campaigns you opted into and your funding share

GST on platform fees18% GST on the platform's own services to youTax invoice from the platform each period
TDS under section 194OIncome tax deducted by the platform on your salesShows in Form 26AS; it is a tax credit, not a cost

One thing is not deducted from you: the GST on the food itself. Since 1 January 2022, GST on restaurant services supplied through e-commerce operators is paid by the platform under section 9(5) of the CGST Act, as explained in CBIC Circular No. 167/23/2021-GST (17 December 2021). The platform issues the invoice for that supply and pays the tax; your own turnover still includes those sales.

What has been reported about commission rates

Neither platform publishes a rate card for restaurants. Everything below comes from news reports, so treat it as a range to compare against, not as your rate.

  • Base commission: Business Standard (17 November 2025), citing CNBC-TV18, said Swiggy's existing commission "typically ranges between 17 and 25 per cent". Deccan Herald described the same range as the standard base for Swiggy.
  • A 30% cap on Zomato service fees: When Zomato added a long-distance fee in May 2025, Business Standard and Inc42 reported that it told restaurants total service fees, including the distance charge, would not cross 30%, "not counting other costs". Some restaurants told reporters their total could go as high as 45%.
  • Zomato long-distance fee (May 2025): ₹15 for deliveries between 4 and 6 km on orders above ₹150, and ₹25–₹35 beyond 6 km depending on the city, as reported by Business Standard and Inc42 citing The Economic Times.
  • Swiggy 2% collection fee (February 2025): Inc42 reported a 2% "collection fee" on orders for select restaurant partners from 16 February 2025, described by Swiggy as a fee "for facilitating seamless customer payments".
  • Swiggy One fee (November 2025): Business Standard reported that select restaurants would pay an additional per-order fee on Swiggy One orders from 25 November 2025, with a blended rate "likely to fall between ₹2 and ₹5 per order", on top of commission.
  • Gross-value base (August 2024): YourStory reported that Swiggy moved a set of non-metro restaurants from net-value to gross-value service fees (gross value includes GST and packaging), noting the change would "marginally increase" the fee for those partners.

The pattern is more important than any single figure: fees change every few months, they differ by city and outlet, and new line items are usually announced by email to selected partners. That is why you need to calculate your own effective rate from your statements, not from a blog's table.

How to calculate your real cost per order

Pick one payout cycle and work through these steps. A spreadsheet is enough. If you already reconcile payouts, our Swiggy and Zomato payout reconciliation checklist gives you the order-level data you need.

  1. Find the fee base. Read the definition in your agreement. Is commission charged on item total, item total plus packaging, or gross value including taxes? The same 22% costs more on a bigger base.
  2. List every percentage fee. Commission, payment or collection fee, and any other percentage line in the statement.
  3. Add per-order fees. Distance fees, programme fees and similar fixed charges. Work out the average per order from the statement, since not every order attracts them.
  4. Add GST on those fees. Platform services to you attract 18% GST. If your restaurant charges GST at 5% without input tax credit, you generally cannot set that GST off, so treat it as a cost. Check this with your accountant.
  5. Add discounts you funded. Only your share, not the platform's.
  6. Add ads. Divide the period's ad spend by the number of orders.
  7. Exclude TDS. Section 194O TDS is a credit against your income tax. ClearTax notes the rate was reduced to 0.1% from 1 October 2024. Track it, claim it, but don't count it as a fee.
  8. Divide by order value. Total deductions divided by menu value of the orders = your effective platform cost.

Then do the step most owners skip: subtract food cost and packaging to see the contribution per order, the money left to pay rent, salaries and profit.

Worked example: a ₹420 delivery order

All figures below are illustrative. They are not Swiggy's or Zomato's actual rates for any restaurant. Replace them with the numbers from your own contract and statement.

Assume a biryani order with an item total of ₹400 and a ₹20 packaging charge. Commission is 22% and the payment fee 2%, both on item total plus packaging. Food cost is 32% of the item price (₹128) and the packaging actually costs you ₹15.

Three things stand out. First, a "22% commission" became 28.3% once the payment fee and GST were added, before any discount. Second, one discount and one distance fee cut the contribution by almost 30% on the same order. Third, the order is still profitable at the contribution level, which is why it rarely makes sense to switch aggregators off. The goal is to know which orders make money and price accordingly.

Price your delivery menu with one formula

If you want a delivery order to leave you the same amount as a counter sale, divide the counter price by (1 - your effective platform cost). Using the illustrative 28.3% above, a dish priced at ₹300 at the counter would need a delivery price of about ₹418 (₹300 divided by 0.717). Most restaurants land somewhere between that and their counter price, and accept a slightly lower margin on delivery in return for volume.

Before you price differently online, check your agreement for any price-parity term. Inc42 reported on 23 April 2026 that Zomato had withdrawn a clause requiring restaurants to match platform prices with dine-in and website prices, and a Zomato source told Business Standard in July 2026 that price parity requirements were removed earlier in 2026. Terms can differ by platform and contract, so read your own.

Is the commission negotiable?

Rates are set per partner, and public reporting shows they have been linked to commercial terms. Business Standard and Business Today, reporting in November 2024 on a confidential Competition Commission of India (CCI) investigation report, said it found Zomato had offered lower commissions to restaurants that listed exclusively, and that Swiggy had offered business-growth guarantees for exclusive listing. Those were investigation findings, not a final CCI order. In July 2026, Business Standard reported that the National Restaurant Association of India (NRAI) had asked the CCI for interim relief against exclusivity and price-parity conditions while the case continues.

Practical points when you talk to your account manager:

  • Bring your own numbers: monthly orders, average order value, ratings and cancellation rate. Volume and reliability are your leverage.
  • Ask what the fee base is and whether packaging is included. A base change can cost more than a one-point rate change.
  • Ask in writing which programme or distance fees apply to your outlet, and how you will be told about changes.
  • Be careful with exclusivity in exchange for a lower rate. Model what happens to your orders if you leave the other platform.
  • Reduce restaurant-funded discounts before asking for a rate cut. They are often the bigger and more controllable cost.

Six ways to protect your margin on delivery

  1. Calculate contribution by dish, not just by order. Low-ticket items like a single coffee or a ₹120 snack can lose money after fixed per-order fees. Bundle them into combos instead.
  2. Raise the average order value. Combos, add-ons and family packs spread fixed per-order fees over a bigger bill.
  3. Cap your delivery radius where your contract allows. If long-distance orders carry extra fees and arrive colder, they may not be worth it.
  4. Run discounts only with a stop date and a target. Measure orders and contribution before and after, not just order count.
  5. Move repeat customers to a direct channel. A customer who reorders every week is worth inviting to your own online ordering website, where the cost is mainly payment and delivery. Use a bill insert or a QR on the packaging, and respect the platform's rules on customer data. New ordering platforms such as Rapido's Ownly, which Business Today reported in July 2026 as operating on a zero-commission model for restaurants, are also worth evaluating, but check the delivery and subscription costs in their terms.
  6. Get all orders into one system. When Swiggy, Zomato, dine-in and direct orders land in the same POS, you can see contribution by channel without copying numbers between tabs. A Swiggy and Zomato POS integration also removes manual punching errors that turn into refunds.

A weekly 15-minute commission check

  • Download the payout statement and the fee invoice for the week.
  • Recalculate effective platform cost (steps above) and compare it with last week.
  • Look for any new line item or a change in the fee base. Ask support immediately if you can't explain it.
  • Check discount and ad spend against the campaigns you approved.
  • Note TDS amounts so your accountant can match them with Form 26AS.
  • Review the five lowest-contribution dishes and decide: reprice, bundle or remove from the delivery menu.

Frequently asked questions

How much commission do Swiggy and Zomato charge restaurants?

Neither platform publishes a rate card; rates are set per outlet in the partner agreement. News reports have described Swiggy's base commission as typically 17–25%, and Zomato told partners in 2025 that total service fees including its distance charge would not cross 30%, excluding other costs.

Is GST charged on Swiggy and Zomato commission?

Yes. The platform's services to the restaurant, such as commission and payment fees, carry 18% GST shown on its tax invoice. Restaurants charging 5% GST without input tax credit generally cannot set this off, so it adds to the real cost; confirm with your accountant.

Do restaurants pay GST on food sold through Swiggy and Zomato?

No. Since 1 January 2022 the platform pays GST on restaurant services supplied through it under section 9(5) of the CGST Act, as clarified in CBIC Circular 167/23/2021-GST. The sales still count in your turnover.

What is the TDS deducted from my Swiggy or Zomato payout?

It is income tax deducted under section 194O, reported to have been reduced to 0.1% from 1 October 2024. It appears in Form 26AS and can be claimed against your income tax, so it is not a platform fee.

Can I charge higher prices on Swiggy and Zomato than in my restaurant?

Check your current agreement first. Inc42 reported in April 2026 that Zomato withdrew a price-parity clause from restaurant contracts, but terms vary by platform and contract, so read the pricing clause in yours.

How can I reduce what I pay to food delivery apps?

Know your effective rate from statements, cut unprofitable restaurant-funded discounts, raise average order value with combos, review long-distance orders, and move repeat customers to your own ordering website or app.
