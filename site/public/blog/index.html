How to choose restaurant POS software

Start by mapping the way your restaurant serves customers. A fine-dine outlet needs table visibility and waiter order taking; a QSR needs a fast counter flow; a cloud kitchen needs order intake and preparation visibility across its brands. Once the service model is clear, compare the actual workflow for order entry, modifiers, KOT, billing, payment and reporting.

Do not compare only the number of features listed on a product page. Ask vendors to demonstrate your real scenarios, explain offline behaviour, identify third-party requirements and provide an itemised quote. Use the POSINEXT pricing page to see the information to prepare for a quote.

Understand the restaurant KOT workflow

A kitchen order ticket (KOT) carries the details the kitchen needs to prepare an order. A useful workflow should preserve the selected items, relevant modifiers and special instructions when an order moves from the POS to the kitchen. During evaluation, confirm how tickets are routed, how staff identify new or changed orders and how the kitchen communicates an issue back to the service team.

Review the Fine Dine POS and QSR POS workflows to see how table and counter orders enter kitchen preparation.

Plan digital ordering without creating disconnected work

Restaurants can accept orders from guests at the table, through a website or app, and from supported delivery marketplaces. Each additional channel should have a defined path into the outlet: who sees the order, who accepts it, how the kitchen receives it and who resolves a customer query. POS-connected ordering can help keep those steps aligned.

Explore QR code ordering, restaurant website ordering, a branded food delivery app and aggregator integration.

Restaurant technology topics to evaluate

  • Billing and taxes: validate bill formats, tax configuration and payment scenarios with your accountant and software provider.
  • Devices and printers: test the actual Windows terminal, printer connection and kitchen devices planned for the outlet.
  • Offline operation: separate local workflows from cloud-dependent features and understand how data sync behaves after reconnection.
  • Staff adoption: walk through common tasks with the people who will use the POS during a busy service.
  • Integrations: confirm account prerequisites, supported order states and any third-party fees before implementation.
