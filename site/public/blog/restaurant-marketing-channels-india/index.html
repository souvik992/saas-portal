How to Get More Restaurant Customers in India

Google Maps, Swiggy and Zomato, your own app, WhatsApp, ONDC, Instagram and Table QR: what each channel costs and how to use them together.

By the POSINEXT team · 19 November 2026

Most restaurant owners in India do not have a demand problem; they have a channel problem. Customers are searching on Google Maps, scrolling Swiggy and Zomato, asking friends on WhatsApp and watching food reels, but each channel brings a different kind of customer at a very different cost. This guide compares the eight channels that genuinely bring customers to Indian restaurants, cafés, QSRs and cloud kitchens in 2026, what each one costs, and how to use them together so that a customer you pay for once keeps coming back for free.

Why your channel mix matters more than ever

Delivery is still growing fast. Redseer's State of India's Food Services Market 2026 report, cited by Business Today, expects online delivery to grow from about 11% of the food services market in FY26 to about 18% by FY31, with the overall market rising from roughly $90 billion in 2025 to nearly $150 billion by 2030. That growth is real, but so is its cost.

In December 2025 the National Council of Applied Economic Research (NCAER) published an impact study based on an in-person survey of 640 restaurants in 28 cities, carried out in 2023. It found that the average per-order commission paid to delivery platforms rose from 9.6% in 2019 to 24.6% in 2023, while the average share of restaurant revenue coming from platforms rose from 22.1% to 28.8%. About half of platform restaurants said platforms increased their customer count, and 35.4% said they would stop using platforms, citing high commissions and insufficient profits among the reasons.

The lesson is not "leave the aggregators". It is: use each channel for the job it does best, and move repeat customers to channels where you keep the margin and the relationship.

1. Google Business Profile and Google Maps

For dine-in, takeaway and cafés, your Google Business Profile is usually the highest-intent channel you have: people searching "biryani near me" or "cafe in Indiranagar" are deciding where to eat right now. Google says local results are based mainly on relevance (how well your profile matches the search), distance and prominence (how well known you are, including your review count and rating, links and mentions across the web, and your position in normal web results).

What to do this week:

  • Claim and verify the profile, and make sure there is only one listing per outlet.
  • Pick the most specific primary category ("South Indian restaurant", not just "Restaurant") and keep hours, holiday hours, phone number and address exactly right.
  • Upload real photos of dishes, the storefront, the dining area and the team, and add new ones every month.
  • Add your menu with prices, and link the "Order online" and website buttons to your own ordering page, not only to an aggregator.
  • Post offers or new dishes regularly and answer questions in the Q&A section.

Use the same name, address and phone number on your website, Swiggy, Zomato, Justdial and social profiles. Inconsistent details make it harder for Google to trust your listing.

2. Reviews and ratings

Reviews are not a separate channel so much as the fuel for every channel: Google uses review count and rating in local ranking, and aggregator ratings affect where you appear in delivery apps. Build a simple habit rather than a campaign:

  • Ask happy guests for a Google review at the moment of delight: when the bill is paid, or with a QR code on the table tent or delivery bag.
  • Reply to every review, positive and negative, within a day or two. Keep replies short, specific and calm; future customers read them.
  • Never buy reviews or offer discounts in exchange for positive ratings. It breaks Google's and the delivery platforms' review policies, and fake or incentivised reviews can get removed or your listing penalised.
  • Feed recurring complaints (cold food, late delivery, portion size) back to the kitchen and fix them; that improves ratings faster than any reply.

3. Swiggy and Zomato: rent their reach, do not depend on it

The aggregators remain the fastest way to reach delivery customers who have never heard of you. In the NCAER survey, visibility (69.1%) and network (56.6%) were the main reasons restaurants stayed on platforms, and 59% said platforms expanded the area they could serve. The trade-off is cost: beyond the base commission there is GST on the commission, payment charges, ads and restaurant-funded discounts. NCAER's pricing example shows a ₹100 dine-in item selling for about ₹138.5 on a platform, with the restaurant receiving about ₹113.8.

How to use them well:

  • Treat aggregators as a customer acquisition channel. Price delivery menus for the real fee structure, and know your contribution per order after all deductions, not just the headline commission.
  • Spend on ads only for items and time slots where the margin can carry it, and track the return weekly.
  • Make the packaging do marketing: a card with your own ordering link or WhatsApp number, and a reason to order direct next time, within each platform's rules.
  • Accept platform orders straight into your POS so they print as KOTs without retyping. See our Swiggy and Zomato POS integration guide.

4. Your own ordering website and app

A direct ordering website or branded app is where repeat delivery and takeaway customers should end up. There is no per-order aggregator commission, you see the customer's name and number, and you can reward loyalty directly. It works best when customers already know you, so the job of every other channel (Google, packaging inserts, Instagram, WhatsApp) is to send them here.

  • Keep the menu identical in items and photos to what customers see elsewhere, and make checkout fast: phone number, address, UPI.
  • Offer a small, sustainable direct-order benefit, such as free delivery above a threshold or a free side, rather than deep discounts.
  • Link it from your Google Business Profile, Instagram bio, WhatsApp Business profile and receipts.

5. WhatsApp: the reorder channel

India is WhatsApp's largest market, with more than 500 million users, and Meta's India leadership has said that about 60% of WhatsApp users interact with businesses every week. For restaurants it is the natural channel for repeat orders, office lunches, bulk and party orders, and festival pre-orders.

  • Set up a WhatsApp Business profile with your hours, address, menu catalogue and ordering link.
  • Collect opt-ins at billing or checkout, and only message people who agreed. Two useful messages a month beat daily spam.
  • Use broadcast lists for genuine news: a new menu, a weekday lunch combo, festival specials.
  • For high volumes, the WhatsApp Business Platform (API) adds automation and catalogues, but conversations are charged, so measure orders per message sent.

6. ONDC and the new low-commission apps

The Open Network for Digital Commerce (ONDC) lets restaurants be discovered through many buyer apps instead of one marketplace. ONDC's own food page claims 100K+ active restaurants and commission structures about 20% lower than conventional platforms. New entrants have also appeared in 2026: Rapido's Ownly launched citywide in Bengaluru in March 2026 with a zero-commission model and a flat customer delivery fee, and Flipkart was reported to be planning a Bengaluru food-delivery pilot on ONDC at around 10% commission.

Be realistic: demand on these networks is still much smaller than on the two big aggregators, and it varies by city. List where onboarding is simple and the orders flow into your POS, keep prices and menus in sync, and judge each one by orders and margin after a month, not by the headline commission.

7. Instagram, reels and local food creators

Short video is how many diners discover new places, especially cafés, dessert shops and launches. It builds awareness rather than immediate orders, so connect it to a channel that converts:

  • Post what is genuinely worth watching: the dish being made, a signature serve, a behind-the-counter moment. Phone video in good light is enough.
  • Put your ordering link, WhatsApp number and Google Maps location in the bio, and mention the area in captions so people know where you are.
  • Work with small local creators whose followers actually live within your delivery radius; ask for a specific dish and a clear call to action, and track it with a code or a dedicated link.

8. In-store capture and Table QR: the cheapest customers are the ones already inside

Every dine-in guest is a customer you have already acquired. Capture them before they walk out:

  • Take the phone number at billing (with consent) so you can send the bill, a feedback link and later offers.
  • Use Table QR ordering so guests can browse and reorder from the table, and so each order is tied to a table and, optionally, a customer.
  • Put a QR code on the table and bill that leads to your Google review page, and another that leads to your direct ordering page.
  • Run a simple loyalty rule your staff can explain in one sentence.

A 30-day plan to get started

  1. Week 1: Fix your Google Business Profile (category, hours, photos, menu, order link) and make your name, address and phone identical everywhere.
  2. Week 2: Start asking for reviews at billing and reply to every review from the last three months.
  3. Week 3: Launch or tidy your direct ordering page and WhatsApp Business profile; add a packaging insert pointing to them.
  4. Week 4: Calculate your real margin per order on each delivery channel, cut ads that do not pay back, and try one low-commission channel if it is live in your city.

Measure every channel in one place

You can only grow the channels you can measure. Track, per channel and per week: orders, average bill, fees and discounts, net margin per order, and how many customers ordered again. Frequently asked questions

What is the best way to get more customers for a restaurant in India?

Combine channels: a complete Google Business Profile and steady reviews for local diners, Swiggy and Zomato to reach new delivery customers, and your own ordering website, WhatsApp and in-store capture to turn those customers into repeat orders you do not pay commission on.

How much commission do Swiggy and Zomato charge restaurants?

It varies by city, contract and restaurant. NCAER's December 2025 study found the average per-order commission rose from 9.6% in 2019 to 24.6% in 2023, and restaurants also pay GST on commission, payment charges, ads and any discounts they fund. Check your own settlement reports for your real cost per order.

Does Google Business Profile help restaurants get customers?

Yes. Google says local results depend mainly on relevance, distance and prominence, and review count and rating are part of prominence. A complete, accurate profile with photos, menu, hours and an order link helps you appear in near-me searches and turns those searches into calls, directions and orders.

Is ONDC worth it for restaurants?

It can lower commission and add reach, but order volumes are still much smaller than on the main aggregators and vary by city. List if onboarding is simple and orders reach your POS, then judge it after a month by orders and margin.

How do I move customers from Swiggy and Zomato to direct ordering?

Make the direct channel easy and worth it: a fast ordering page or app, a small sustainable benefit such as free delivery above a threshold, and reminders on your Google profile, Instagram bio, WhatsApp and packaging, within each platform's rules.
