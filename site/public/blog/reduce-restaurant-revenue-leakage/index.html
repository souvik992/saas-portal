How to Reduce Revenue Leakage in Your Restaurant: 8 Controls

Revenue leakage in restaurants: cancelled items after KOT, voids, reprints, unbilled food and cash out. Eight POS controls that stop money slipping away.

By the POSINEXT team · 13 November 2026

Most restaurants lose money not through one big theft but through many small leaks: an item cancelled after it was cooked, a bill voided and re-rung lower, food that left the kitchen and never reached a bill. The fix is not suspicion; it is a POS that makes every sensitive action visible.

Where leakage happens

  • Cancelled after KOT. A dish is fired to the kitchen, then removed from the order.
  • Voids and re-billing. A printed bill is voided and the table billed again for less.
  • Reprints. A bill printed twice, one copy paid and one quietly settled.
  • Discounts and complimentary items beyond what was approved.
  • Cash out of the drawer without a reason.
  • Food made, never paid. KOTs with no matching bill.
  • Stock that does not add up against recipes.

8 controls that stop it

  1. Personal PINs for everyone. No shared logins, so every action has a name.
  2. Manager PIN for sensitive actions. Voids, cancellations after KOT, reprints and discounts above a role's limit need approval.
  3. Reasons on every change. Picked from a list, so they can be counted.
  4. Change slips to the kitchen. When an item is reduced or cancelled, the station gets a slip, so the kitchen knows and can push back.
  5. A leakage dashboard. Counts and amounts of each kind of leak, with a drill-down to who, when and why.
  6. An audit log you cannot edit. Every change kept, with paging and search.
  7. Recipe-based stock. Compare what recipes say you used with what you bought.
  8. Day-end checks. Cash counted against the Z-report, with the difference shown.

Frequently asked questions

What is revenue leakage in a restaurant?

Money lost through small gaps: items cancelled after KOT, voided or reprinted bills, unapproved discounts, cash taken out of the drawer and food that was made but never billed.

How can a POS reduce leakage?

By giving every staff member their own PIN, requiring a manager's PIN for sensitive actions, recording reasons, printing change slips to the kitchen and showing it all on a leakage dashboard and audit log.
