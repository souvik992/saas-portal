Google Business Profile for Restaurants in India: Setup Checklist

Set up, verify and rank your restaurant's Google Business Profile in India: categories, menu, order links, photos, reviews and a 30-day checklist.

POSINEXT Editorial Team19 November 2026 · 12 min read

When someone in your neighbourhood types "biryani near me", "cafe open now" or just your restaurant's name into Google, the first thing they usually see is not your website or your Swiggy page. It is your Google Business Profile: the panel on Google Search and Maps with your photos, rating, timings, menu, phone number and an order button. For many diners that panel is the whole decision.

This guide walks an Indian restaurant, café, QSR or cloud kitchen owner through setting up that profile properly, getting it verified, filling every field Google actually uses, and keeping it working week after week. Every rule below comes from Google's own help pages, read on the date of publishing and linked at the end. Google changes features often and some options vary by country and business, so treat your own dashboard as the final word. If you have not read it yet, start with our overview of the 8 channels that bring restaurant customers in India; this post goes deep on the first one.

Why your Google profile matters more in 2026

Google says local results are based mainly on three things: relevance (how well your profile matches what was searched), distance (how far you are from the searcher) and prominence (how well known you are, based partly on links and on reviews). Its help page adds that "more reviews and positive ratings can help your business's local ranking" and is clear that there is no way to request or pay for a better local ranking. You cannot change distance, so the work is in relevance and prominence.

The profile is also feeding Google's AI features. On 12 March 2026 Google announced that Ask Maps, a Gemini-powered way to ask Maps conversational questions, was rolling out in India in English, with Hindi to follow. Google says Maps analyses information from over 300 million places, including reviews from more than 500 million contributors, and its Indian example suggests vegetarian-friendly places based on a user's preferences. When a diner asks Maps for "a quiet place for a family dinner with parking", the facts you publish and the details customers write in reviews are what Google has to work with.

Finally, trust is not automatic. A LocalCircles survey reported by Business Standard (December 2022, over 56,000 responses from 357 districts) found that 46% of respondents had low or zero trust in Google ratings and reviews. That is a reason to do this properly: recent, specific, genuine reviews and polite owner replies are what make a profile believable to a sceptical Indian diner.

Step 1: Claim or create the profile

  1. Search your restaurant's name and area on Google Maps. If a listing already exists, use Claim this business instead of creating a duplicate. Google's guidelines say not to create more than one profile for each location.
  2. If nothing exists, go to business.google.com and add the business. Use a Google account the business controls long term, not a staff member's personal Gmail, and add a second manager later so you are never locked out.
  3. Enter the name exactly as it appears on your signboard, menu and bills (see the name rules below).
  4. Choose your primary category, add your address and pin, phone number and website or ordering page.

Step 2: Get verified (and how not to delay it)

Photos and posts only go live after verification, and Google says verification makes you more likely to appear in results. Google decides which methods you are offered; you cannot pick. Some businesses need more than one.

MethodWhat Google asks forWatch out for
Phone call or SMSA code sent to the business numberAn IVR system will not receive the code; keep a person on that line
EmailAccess to the email shown on screenUse an inbox the owner controls
Live video callShow location, business and proof you manage it, live, during opening hoursNot offered to every business
Video recordingAn unedited live recording of at least 30 seconds, uploaded from the phoneReview can take up to 5 business days
PostcardA five-digit code mailed to your addressUsually arrives within 14 days and expires after 30 days

For video verification of a storefront, Google asks you to show your location (street signs, building number or neighbouring shops), your business name "clearly printed on a permanent fixture" that matches the profile, and proof of management. Google's own examples of proof include opening a cash register or using a point-of-sale system, or going into a storage room. So shoot the video during service: walk from the main road to your signboard, go inside, open the billing screen and the store room. Do not show bank, tax or ID numbers, and avoid other people's faces.

If you get a postcard, do not edit your name, address or category while you wait, and do not request a second code, because Google says both can invalidate or delay the first one.

Step 3: Fill every field the way Google wants

Business name

Your name should match your real-world name. Google's guidelines do not allow taglines, phone numbers, URLs, timings, all-caps words, or product or location details stuffed into the name. "Sharma Dhaba" is fine; "Sharma Dhaba Best Punjabi Thali Near Station Open 24 Hours" is not. Relevance should come from your categories, menu, description and reviews instead.

Categories

Google asks you to use "as few categories as possible" and to pick ones that finish the sentence "this business is a…", not "this business has a…". Choose a specific primary category ("South Indian restaurant", "Biryani restaurant", "Cafe", "Bakery") over a generic "Restaurant", then add only the secondary categories that are genuinely true. A café that sells a few sandwiches is not a "Sandwich shop" as well.

Address, service area and cloud kitchens

Dine-in and takeaway outlets show their precise address. Cloud kitchens need care. Google allows delivery-only brands only if they have distinct branded packaging and a distinct website; such brands must add service areas and hide the address. If several brands run from one kitchen, each needs its own profile (do not combine brand names into one), and Google says multiple virtual brands at one location face additional verification. Show an address only if you offer pickup to all customers. If you run delivery brands, our cloud kitchen software page covers running several brands from one kitchen.

Hours

For restaurants, Google's guideline is specific: use the hours when diners can sit down and eat. If you have no dine-in, use takeaway hours, and delivery hours only as a last resort. Add special hours for Diwali, Christmas, Eid, Holi and local holidays in advance.

Phone and website

Use a number that reaches this outlet and is under the business's direct control, not a central call centre. Google does not allow numbers or URLs that simply redirect people elsewhere, including to a social media page. Link your own website or ordering page.

Attributes and description

Fill the attributes Google offers you (dine-in, takeaway, delivery, outdoor seating, Wi-Fi, parking, payment options and so on). People now ask Maps about exactly these details. In the description, say plainly what you serve, your speciality and your area, in normal sentences.

Step 4: Put your menu and ordering on the profile

Food businesses get a menu editor: sections (starters, mains, desserts), items, descriptions and prices. Google says changes can take 24–48 hours to show on Maps and Search. You can also upload menu photos or a PDF, and add a menu link under Business information. Two guideline points catch many owners out:

  • The menu must represent what is actually available. Sample menus that list only "popular items" should not be submitted.
  • Menu URLs cannot be direct links to third-party ordering or delivery services. Link your own menu page instead.

Ordering is managed separately under Food ordering in your profile. Third-party providers that say they have an authorised relationship with you can appear automatically. You can hide providers, add your own ordering link, and mark one link as preferred for pickup, delivery or both. Google says changes usually appear within a few hours, and that in some regions options appear under an "Order online" button while elsewhere they show as separate links, so check what your own profile shows.

This is the single biggest money decision on the profile. If your preferred link is an aggregator, every hungry person who finds you on Google pays commission. If it is your own online ordering website, you keep the customer and the margin. Many owners keep the aggregators listed but set their own link as preferred.

Step 5: Photos and short videos

Google's photo specs: JPG or PNG, between 10 KB and 5 MB, recommended 720 × 720 pixels (minimum 250 × 250), sharp, well lit and without heavy filters. Videos can be up to 30 seconds, up to 75 MB, at 720p or higher. New uploads can take 24–48 hours to appear and only show after verification.

A good starting set for a restaurant:

  • Logo and a cover photo
  • The entrance and signboard, by day and at night, so people can find you
  • Seating areas (family section, outdoor, private dining if you have it)
  • Your 8–10 best-selling dishes, shot in daylight on your real plates
  • A 15–30 second video of the space during service

Add a few new photos every month, especially when the menu changes.

Step 6: Reviews, the right way

Reviews feed prominence and give Ask Maps the details it summarises. Google suggests sharing your review link or a QR code with customers at checkout or in follow-up messages. Practical places in an Indian restaurant: a QR card on the table or at the billing counter, a line on the printed bill, and a WhatsApp message after a delivery or a large party order.

What you must not do: Google's policies ban offering anything for a review, including discounts, free items or other benefits, and also ban rewarding people to change or remove reviews. "Show us your 5-star review and get a free dessert" breaks the rules. Google says profiles caught with fake engagement can have new reviews blocked, existing reviews unpublished for a period, and a public warning shown to customers that fake reviews were removed.

How to reply, following Google's own tips:

  • Keep replies short, polite and personal; avoid the same copy-paste "Thank you!" on every review.
  • For a complaint, use the reviewer's name, acknowledge the specific issue, apologise where it is deserved and say what you changed.
  • Move complicated issues to phone or email; never share a customer's private details or argue.
  • Do not use replies to advertise offers.

A short template: "Hi Priya, sorry the paneer tikka arrived cold on Saturday. We have changed how we pack tandoor items for delivery. Please call us on the number on our profile next time and ask for the manager. We'd like to make it right." (Priya is an example name, not a real review.)

Step 7: Posts and offers

Posts let you publish updates, offers (with dates, an optional coupon code and terms) and events. You can schedule posts or set them to repeat. Google archives posts older than six months unless you set a date range, and posts with a phone number in the description may be rejected. Ideas: a seasonal menu, a festival thali, a live-music night or a weekday lunch combo.

Step 8: Measure what the profile does

Your profile's Performance report shows the metrics that apply to you, including total interactions, the search terms people used to find you, calls, direction requests, website clicks, bookings (if you use a booking provider) and menu clicks. Check it once a month and compare it with your own sales data: are calls turning into takeaway orders, are website clicks turning into online orders? A POS that records the order source makes this comparison much easier.

Frequently asked questions

Is Google Business Profile free for restaurants in India?

Yes. Creating, verifying and managing a Business Profile is free, and Google says there is no way to pay for a better local ranking. Google Ads are a separate, paid product.

How long does Google Business Profile verification take?

It depends on the method Google offers you. Phone, SMS and email can be quick; Google says video recordings can take up to five business days to review, and postcards usually arrive within 14 days.

Can a cloud kitchen have a Google Business Profile?

Yes, if each delivery-only brand has distinct branded packaging and a distinct website. Such brands must add service areas and hide the address, and each brand needs its own profile rather than one combined listing.

Can I link my Swiggy or Zomato page as my menu link on Google?

No. Google's guidelines say menu URLs cannot be direct links to third-party ordering or delivery services. Aggregators can still appear under Food ordering, where you can also add your own ordering link and set it as preferred.

Can I give customers a discount for leaving a Google review?

No. Google's policies ban offering discounts, free items or any other benefit in exchange for reviews. Profiles found with fake engagement can have new reviews blocked and a warning shown to customers.

Should I add keywords like 'best biryani' to my restaurant name on Google?

No. Google requires the name to match your real-world signboard name and does not allow extra keywords, taglines or location details. Put those details in your categories, menu, description and posts instead.
