Direct Online Ordering for Restaurants: Website vs App vs Swiggy

Direct online ordering for restaurants in India: compare an ordering website, your own app and Swiggy/Zomato on cost, data and reach, with a ₹ example.

POSINEXT Editorial Team22 November 2026 · 10 min read

Every restaurant owner who pays an aggregator bill eventually asks the same question: should we take online orders ourselves? The honest answer is that most restaurants in India need both. Swiggy and Zomato bring new customers you could not reach alone. Your own ordering website or app keeps the customers who already know you, at a far lower cost per order. The skill is deciding which orders belong where, and moving the right ones without losing sales.

This guide compares the three options (an ordering website, your own app and aggregators) on cost, effort, customer data and reach, with a worked example in rupees and a 30-day plan. It is the direct-ordering chapter of our guide to restaurant marketing channels in India.

Why direct ordering matters more every year

Online delivery is still a small slice of what Indians spend on eating out, and it is growing fast. Redseer's State of India's Food Services Market 2026, as reported by Business Today in July 2026, puts online delivery at 11% of India's food services market in FY26, rising to about 18% by FY31. A separate Investec Equities report covered by ANI in May 2026 estimated online food delivery at ₹790 billion in FY25 and projected ₹1.7–2.2 trillion by FY30. Different firms measure this differently, but they agree on the direction: more of your sales will come through a screen.

At the same time, the cost of the aggregator channel has crept up on both sides:

  • For restaurants: Business Standard reported in November 2025, citing CNBC-TV18, that Swiggy's commission typically ranges between 17% and 25%, and that Swiggy began charging select partners an extra ₹2–5 per Swiggy One order from 25 November 2025, over and above existing commission.
  • For customers: YourStory reported in March 2026 that Zomato raised its customer platform fee from ₹12.5 to ₹14.9 per order (before taxes) and Swiggy raised its fee to ₹17.58 (including GST). Both fees started at ₹2 in 2023.

Rates vary by city, outlet and contract, so your own agreement and payout statements are the only reliable source for your numbers. Our Swiggy and Zomato commission guide shows how to work out your effective rate line by line.

Meanwhile, the market is opening up. Rapido's zero-commission Ownly, which Business Today reported in July 2026 had moved inside the main Rapido app with close to 20,000 restaurants listed, and the government-backed ONDC network both give restaurants lower-cost ways to reach customers. And Zomato's food delivery CEO Aditya Mangla said in November 2025 (Inc42) that Zomato was in early stages of an opt-in feature that would share a customer's phone number with a restaurant only if the customer consents. Check the current status in your partner app before you plan around it.

Ordering website: the first step for almost everyone

A good ordering website opens in any phone browser with no install, which matters: a customer who scans a QR code on your bag or taps a link on your Google listing should be able to order in under a minute. It is also the easiest direct channel to market, because one link works on Google Business Profile, Instagram bio, WhatsApp messages and printed inserts. Our Google Business Profile guide explains how to add your own order link to your listing.

Look for: your own domain or sub-domain, a menu that syncs with your POS (so a dish marked out of stock disappears everywhere), delivery zones with minimum order and delivery charge by distance, UPI and card payments, cash on delivery if you want it, and orders that print a KOT automatically instead of being re-typed.

Your own app: worth it only with a loyal base

An app gives you a permanent icon on the customer's phone and push notifications, which are powerful for regulars. But nobody installs an app for a restaurant they have ordered from once. Apple's Developer Program costs US$99 a year (Apple notes that in India enrolment is through the Apple Developer app, with prices shown in local currency), and Google Play charges a US$25 one-time registration fee, before any development or software cost. Start with the website; add an app when you have a base of customers who order every week, such as office regulars, tiffin subscribers or a strong neighbourhood brand.

Aggregators: keep them for discovery

Swiggy and Zomato are where hungry people browse. Leaving them usually means losing sales you cannot replace, especially for a new outlet or a cloud kitchen. The practical goal is not to quit, but to make sure aggregator orders are profitable on their own (price your delivery menu with the full fee load in mind) and to give satisfied aggregator customers a reason to order directly next time.

Zero-commission platforms and ONDC

These sit between the two. ONDC's food and beverages page says restaurants reach customers through participating buyer apps and claims much lower commission structures than conventional platforms, but it publishes no fee schedule for restaurants: your costs depend on the seller app you join through and any logistics charges. Ownly says restaurants pay zero commission. Treat both as extra channels to test in your city, and track their actual cost per order the same way you track Swiggy and Zomato. We cover ONDC in detail in a separate guide.

The delivery rate uses Shiprocket Quick's published "starting at ₹10/km" price; actual quotes depend on distance, city, time and weight, and your own riders may be cheaper or costlier.

Three lessons from the arithmetic:

  1. Delivery is the biggest direct cost. Direct ordering pays best for short-distance orders and pickup. Set a delivery radius and a delivery fee by distance.
  2. You do not need to match the aggregator's discounts. A modest, restaurant-funded reward for ordering direct still leaves a margin advantage.
  3. A small shift is enough. You do not need half your orders to come direct. Moving your regulars is where the money is.

How to move customers to direct ordering (without breaking rules)

Read your aggregator agreement first: clauses on menu pricing and on contacting customers who ordered through the platform differ by platform and contract, and they can change. Then use channels you control:

  1. Dine-in and takeaway customers. Put a QR code for your ordering website on table tents, bills and the counter. These customers already like you and cost nothing to reach.
  2. Google. Add your order link to your Google Business Profile and website so people searching your name order directly.
  3. Packaging. A small insert or sticker with a QR code and a clear reason ("order direct for free dessert on orders above ₹500") reaches people who already liked your food enough to eat it. Keep it within your aggregator contract.
  4. WhatsApp and Instagram. Share the ordering link in your bio, stories and WhatsApp Business catalogue. Send offers only to people who have opted in.
  5. A loyalty reason. Points, a stamp card or a "regulars' price" on a few dishes. Direct customers should feel a small, consistent benefit.

What to look for in direct-ordering software

  • One menu, one POS. Website, app, Swiggy and Zomato orders should land in the same POS and print KOTs automatically. Re-typing orders is slow and causes mistakes.
  • Stock sync. Mark an item unavailable once and it disappears from every channel.
  • No per-order commission. A fixed subscription is easier to plan around than a percentage of every order.
  • Payments and reconciliation. UPI and cards through a gateway, cash on delivery if you allow it, and reports that match payouts to bills.
  • Customer data you own. Order history by customer, so you can see who your regulars are and what they buy.
  • Works when the internet drops. In-store billing and KOTs should keep running during an outage.

A 30-day plan to launch direct ordering

  • Week 1: Pull three months of aggregator statements and calculate your effective cost per order. Decide your delivery radius, delivery fee and whether you offer pickup.
  • Week 2: Set up the ordering website, payment gateway and delivery partner. Place ten test orders, including a cancellation and a refund.
  • Week 3: Add the link to Google, Instagram and WhatsApp. Print QR codes for tables, bills and the counter. Brief staff on one line to say at billing.
  • Week 4: Track direct orders, cost per order, repeat rate and complaints. Keep what works, and drop delivery zones that lose money.

Tax note: GST treatment of orders through e-commerce operators differs from orders on your own website. Check the rules at cbic.gov.in and confirm with your accountant. This is general information, not tax or legal advice.

Frequently asked questions

Should my restaurant leave Swiggy and Zomato if I start direct ordering?

Usually not. Aggregators bring customers who have never heard of you. Keep them for discovery, make sure each aggregator order is profitable, and use your own website to serve regulars at a lower cost per order.

Is an ordering website or an app better for a restaurant in India?

Start with a website: it needs no install and works from Google, Instagram, WhatsApp and QR codes. Add an app once you have regulars who order every week, since an app also carries Apple and Google developer fees.

What does a direct online order cost a restaurant?

Mainly the payment gateway fee (Razorpay's standard rate is 2% plus 18% GST on the fee), delivery, and any discount you offer for ordering direct, plus your software subscription. Delivery is usually the largest part, so set a delivery radius and fee.

Can I ask Swiggy or Zomato customers to order directly from me?

Read your partner agreement first, because terms on pricing and customer contact vary by platform and contract. Focus on channels you control, such as dine-in QR codes, Google, Instagram and opted-in WhatsApp lists.

How do I deliver orders from my own website?

Use your own riders or a hyperlocal delivery partner. Shiprocket Quick, for example, lists food delivery starting at ₹10 per km in several cities; get quotes for your area and distance before setting your delivery fee.
